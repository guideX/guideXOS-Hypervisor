SSI ownership and architectural behavior
----------------------------------------

- In the existing RV64CPU implementation, S-mode sip writes are limited to
  SSIP when MIDELEG delegates it. sie.SSIE is independently gated by MIDELEG;
  sstatus.SIE controls same-privilege S-mode delivery. SIP/STIP and SIP/SEIP
  remain device/M-mode owned. Trap entry does not clear SSIP.
- The fixture guest sets SSIP with an S-mode CSRRS sip instruction, reads the
  bit back, and clears it with CSRRC sip in the SSI handler. Guest records and
  test checks prove the clear removes SSIP while preserving other supervisor
  pending bits. Focused CPU checks also prove SSIP and machine MSIP are
  independent and that MSIP remains nondelegable.
- MIDELEG exposed the supported supervisor mask 0x222 for causes 1, 5, and 9.
  A focused nondelegated-SSIP case confirmed an S-mode sip write cannot assert
  the bit. S-mode access to MIDELEG and an unimplemented CSR trapped as illegal
  instructions. An enabled machine MSIP preempted S-mode into M-mode.
- No host-side SSI source, SBI interception, ACLINT MSWI, SBI IPI, remote-hart
  signaling, or SMP support was added. Local SSIP CSR testing does not
  demonstrate SBI IPI or inter-hart messaging.

Three-way and four-source arbitration evidence
----------------------------------------------

- The guest log contains 20 real S-mode handler entries and records sequence,
  scause, sepc, sip, sie, sstatus, device/acknowledgment evidence, and timer
  deadline/rearm evidence. The test reads the log from guest RAM and compares
  every cause and service item with the expected sequence.
- Three guest-owned pre-enable snapshots recorded sip=sie=0x222 with global
  SIE clear. Four live preselection observations captured actual CPU CSR and
  device state. Representative values:

    phase 1 first claim: mip=0x2a2 sip=0x222 sie=0x222 sstatus=0x2
      PLIC pending=0x1400; source 10 priority=1, source 12 priority=3
    phase 1 second claim: mip=0x2a2 sip=0x222 sie=0x222 sstatus=0x22
      PLIC pending=0x0400 after source 12 was claimed/completed
    phase 2: mip=0x2a2 sip=0x222 sie=0x222 sstatus=0x22 PLIC=0x0400
    phase 3: mip=0x2a2 sip=0x222 sie=0x222 sstatus=0x22 PLIC=0x1000

- At each simultaneous boundary the next CPU step selected cause 9 and left
  the interrupted instruction unretired. The handler order was:

    four-source: SEI/source 12, SEI/source 10, SSI, STI
    three-way 2: SEI/source 10, SSI, STI
    three-way 3: SEI/source 12, SSI, STI

- The four-source case distinguishes PLIC source priority (12 before 10) from
  architectural cause priority (SEI before SSI before STI). The still-pending
  PLIC source kept SEIP asserted after the first claim. The three-way runs
  preserved the same interrupted PC across consecutive entries until all
  three causes were serviced. Numeric cause order was not used as a priority
  substitute; production priority code was left unchanged.
- The bounded event order across the full guest is:

    9,9,1,5, 9,1,5, 9,1,5, 9,5,1, 9,1,5, 1,5,9, 5

  The cause values in guest RAM include the architectural interrupt flag.
  Counts are seven SEI, six SSI, seven STI, seven PLIC claims/completions, and
  20 SRETs. Both UART sources supplied guest-read bytes.

Masking, WFI, acknowledgment, and timer service
-----------------------------------------------

- SSIE masking: guest SSIP remained visible while SSIE was clear. SEI and STI
  ran first; the original SSI was delivered after SSIE was restored.
- STIE masking: SEI and SSI ran while STIP remained pending. Re-enabling STIE
  delivered the original timer request.
- SEIE masking: SSI and STI ran while UART0 data and its PLIC request remained
  pending. Re-enabling SEIE delivered it without reinjecting the byte.
- Global SIE masking: all three pending identities remained set while S-mode
  trap delivery was deferred. WFI with those pending sources returned without
  a mandatory trap; the later eligible boundary selected SEI first.
- WFI executed three times and woke twice. The first wait began with SSIP
  pending but all local supervisor enables clear. UART0/source 10,
  UART1/source 12, and MTIP were made available before the wake. MTIP entered
  M-mode/OpenSBI first; the supervisor snapshot was taken only after OpenSBI
  presented STIP. The second wait had no locally enabled S interrupt and woke
  through the authentic timer path.
- The guest serviced timers only after authentic SBI TIME calls. The integrated
  run recorded 14 SBI TIME calls, 15 mtimecmp writes, seven MTIP edges, seven
  OpenSBI STIP presentations, and seven STIP clears/rearms. No host-side SBI
  provider was used.
- UART data was injected through the existing deterministic receive interface.
  The guest performed all PLIC claims/completions and UART RBR reads using
  translated S-mode MMIO. SSI handling did not consume UART data or complete a
  PLIC source.

Remaining Linux prerequisites and RISCV-15 recommendation
--------------------------------------------------------

- Linux and U-Boot remain out of scope. The platform is single-hart and still
  lacks PMP, SBI HSM/IPI/RFENCE, full interrupt-controller compatibility,
  virtio, PCI, storage, networking, and a full UART FIFO/modem implementation.
  A Linux boot target also needs an explicit kernel/Image entry contract,
  memory reservations, root-filesystem storage, and a selected console/device
  configuration.
- Recommended RISCV-15: define and validate the next single-hart protection
  boundary, including PMP permissions over the firmware, guest RAM, and MMIO
  regions and their interaction with Sv39 access faults. Keep SBI IPI and
  multicore work deferred until a separate multi-hart phase authorizes it.
