Interrupt arbitration and simultaneous-pending evidence
-------------------------------------------------------

- At the first simultaneous boundary (scenario A), the retained trace records
  S-mode PC/sepc 0x802001E0, `mip=0x2A0`, `sip=0x220`, `sie=0x220`, and
  `sstatus=0x2`. Thus `mip` contains MTIP+SEIP and `sip` contains STIP+SEIP;
  both supervisor local enables and global SIE are set. PLIC source 10 is
  pending, its S-context output is asserted, and
  source priorities are 1/3 with threshold 0. Both supervisor requests are
  eligible at that boundary.
- Cause 9 is selected first at the same PC without retiring the interrupted
  instruction. The S-mode external handler claims source 10 and reads byte
  0x41. Cause 5 is then taken at the same saved EPC; the UART source and data
  are not consumed by timer handling. The guest records SEI then STI.
- Four explicit simultaneous-boundary checks all select cause 9: scenario A,
  scenario D after M-mode timer forwarding, and both external claims in
  scenario G. Each check verifies the selected target is S-mode, `sepc` equals
  the preempted PC, and the instruction-retired counter did not advance.
- In scenario G, both PLIC source 10 and source 12 are pending while STIP and
  SEIP are enabled. PLIC priority selects source 12 (priority 3), then source
  10 (priority 1). At both arbitration boundaries the CPU selects SEI before
  the still-pending STI. After both PLIC claims/completions, the guest services
  the timer. This distinguishes PLIC arbitration between external sources
  from architectural arbitration between SEIP and STIP.
- The privilege ordering is visible in scenario D: S-mode WFI waits with
  global SIE masked; UART1 input and the bounded time advance make both MTIP
  and PLIC/SEIP pending. MTIP first traps to M-mode (cause 7), as a higher
  privilege interrupt. Authentic OpenSBI handles it and presents STIP. Once
  S-mode delivery is enabled, SEI is selected before STI. The WFI wake is
  recorded separately from the S-mode interrupt entries.

Masking, WFI, and event log
--------------------------

- Scenario B keeps STIP pending with STIE clear while UART1/SEIP is serviced;
  enabling STIE later delivers the original timer event.
- Scenario C keeps UART0 pending with SEIE clear while STI is serviced. The
  PLIC pending bit and UART byte remain available; enabling SEIE later delivers
  the original external event.
- Scenario E raises the PLIC threshold to 2, masking source 10 at priority 1
  while the timer proceeds. The pending bit and UART byte survive. Lowering the
  threshold delivers the source without reinjection.
- Scenario F disables UART1 receive IER while a byte is present. Timer service
  proceeds while UART data is retained; reenabling IER then asserts the PLIC
  source and the guest reads the original byte.
- Focused CPU checks distinguish WFI waiting, wake, and trap: masked pending
  STIP does not fabricate a trap; MTIP wakes WFI and traps to M-mode; UART-only
  SEIP wakes WFI but global SIE can defer S-mode delivery until enabled. The
  mixed guest uses a bounded virtual-time jump while waiting and a finite
  instruction budget plus repeated-PC guard.
- Guest log order (interrupt causes shown as names):

    A: SEI source 10 byte 0x41; STI
    B: SEI source 12 byte 0x61; STI
    C: STI; SEI source 10 byte 0x42
    D: SEI source 12 byte 0x62; STI
    E: STI; SEI source 10 byte 0x43
    F: STI; SEI source 12 byte 0x63
    G: SEI source 12 byte 0x64; SEI source 10 byte 0x44; STI

Remaining scope and next phase
------------------------------

- Linux remains out of scope. The implementation still has one hart and lacks
  PMP, SBI HSM/IPI/RFENCE, APLIC/IMSIC, virtio, PCI, storage, networking, and
  full UART FIFO/modem behavior. Linux would also need a selected kernel/image
  and root-filesystem boot contract, memory reservations, and devices for its
  console and storage.
- Recommended RISCV-14: exercise the remaining middle-priority SSI case using
  a narrow single-hart software-interrupt source, including clear and WFI
  behavior, then prove the complete SEI > SSI > STI three-way arbitration.
  Keep authentic firmware boundaries where applicable and avoid SMP or a
  general IPI subsystem unless that phase explicitly requires it.
